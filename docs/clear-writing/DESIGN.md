# Clear writing: design

This package applies to English nonfiction: drafting, revision, explanation,
and critique. The requested transformation controls what may change. A faithful
edit must retain the source’s meaning; a new explanation may select facts
according to the reader’s needs. Creative writing, verbatim transcription, and
code-only work are outside its ordinary scope.

The central requirement is that clarity must not come from changing what the
writer can honestly claim. Scope, actors, attribution, uncertainty, negation,
conditions, and technical identifiers can all carry meaning. The package
therefore establishes the task and preservation boundary before choosing
support, arranging paragraphs, or revising sentences.

A short list of sentence rules would be easier to remember, but could encourage
active voice, brevity, or simple words at the expense of meaning. This package
instead treats Williams-inspired principles as diagnostics. That requires more
judgment and context; it avoids treating one sentence pattern or vocabulary
list as universally correct. Clear prose can still be wrong, so factual
investigation and publication authority remain separate.

The source reference explains the Williams-inspired basis and the limits of
that attribution. It does not claim a full scholarly reconstruction or a
measured improvement for this release. Evidence that a legitimate faithful edit
loses an important qualification, that useful repetition is systematically
removed, or that ordinary tasks require reconstructing unavailable private
context would reopen the design. Any proposed revision must preserve the
original task’s commitments, not merely produce shorter prose.
