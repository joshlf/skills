# Reader-oriented explanation: design

This package applies to technical explanations whose readers do not share the
writer’s full context. It covers selecting and connecting facts in
documentation, comments, reports, commit messages, and similar prose.
Formatting-only edits and independent technical investigation are outside its
scope.

The explanation owes the reader a supported understanding appropriate to the
requested artifact. Establish the transformation, audience, and preservation
boundary; then include the facts and relationships needed to understand,
verify, or decide. An expert may still lack subsystem context. A useful private
briefing is not automatically shared reader knowledge or appropriate
publication content.

Covering every discovered fact would overwhelm the reader, while minimizing
words could remove a necessary premise or qualification. The package selects
material by what it contributes to this reader’s task, subject to required
completeness. Source fidelity can require retaining uncertainty that a smoother
story would resolve. A missing factual premise calls for evidence or
qualification, not more persuasive connective prose.

The method relies on domain-specific evidence and authority from elsewhere; it
cannot investigate facts or approve publication by itself. Evidence that
readers routinely infer a stronger claim than the support allows, that a
faithful transformation drops a separate obligation, or that writer-only
history crowds out the needed mechanism would reopen the guidance. Checks
should examine the whole account and its actual reader context rather than
rewarding a preferred sentence form.

See [evidence and limits](../EVIDENCE.md) for the scope of public validation.
