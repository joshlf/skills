# Model refinement: design

This package applies to conceptual models in software, protocols, schemas,
configuration, instructions, and theories. It covers simplification and useful
generalization; cosmetic shortening and neural-network compression are outside
its scope. No named peer skill is required.

The governing contract comes before a candidate representation. A
simplification must preserve required observations and constraints; a
generalization must retain the claimed old domain while explaining worthwhile
new cases. Count complexity in consumers, mappings, validation, and operator
duties as well as in the visible core. A smaller artifact is not sufficient
evidence of a simpler model.

One pass is cheaper than iteration, but implementing or checking a candidate
can expose a different conceptual problem. Focused work therefore revisits the
model after accepted conceptual changes and rereads the established method at
pass boundaries. Opportunity awareness is deliberately lighter: notice a useful
possibility during other work without launching an unrelated redesign. These
different modes prevent a background observation from silently becoming an
open-ended project.

The method assumes the relevant contract and evidence can be recovered well
enough to assess the claim. Missing checks or unresolved meaning can require a
bounded stop. Local convergence is conditional on scope and evidence; it
neither proves a global minimum nor grants deployment authority. Evidence that
the stopping rule routinely hides unresolved preservation obligations, or that
the method imposes extra structures without a task benefit, would reopen it.
Self-revision must still be judged under the governing method, not under a
candidate that removes its own obligations.
